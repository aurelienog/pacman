# Technical Challenges & Lessons Learned

Throughout the development of the project, several technical challenges emerged while implementing the game engine and integrating the rendering system. Although none of these issues blocked the project completely, they required architectural decisions and multiple design iterations before reaching a stable solution.

## Technical Challenges

| Area                                     | Challenge                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Resolution                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Outcome                                                                                                                                          |
| ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Rendering & Gameplay Synchronization** | Collision detection did not visually match the rendered positions because the game engine operates on a grid while the renderer interpolates entity movement. This caused collisions to appear either too early or too late from the player's perspective. | Several alternatives were evaluated, including interpolated hitboxes, continuous collision detection and a shared render timeline. The final implementation preserves the discrete simulation while delaying cell ownership through an interpolation threshold, improving visual consistency without modifying the game logic. | Collision perception is significantly closer to the visual representation while preserving deterministic gameplay and a simple collision system. |
| **Gameplay Fairness**                    | Purely grid-based collision detection occasionally felt unfair because entities could visually appear not to overlap despite already occupying the same logical tile.                                                                                      | The collision system now favours the player's visual perception by delaying tile occupation until the entity has progressed sufficiently into the destination cell, while frightened ghost collisions remain strictly cell-based to ensure responsive gameplay.                                                                | The resulting gameplay feels more intuitive without increasing the complexity of the AI or collision algorithms.                                 |
| **Ghost AI & Pathfinding** | The initial ghost movement relied only on Manhattan distance to select the next move. Although simple, this approach frequently produced unintelligent behaviour, with ghosts getting trapped, taking poor routes and failing to resemble the behaviour of the original Pac-Man ghosts. | The movement system was redesigned using **Breadth-First Search (BFS)** to compute shortest paths on the maze. On top of this, each ghost was given its own targeting strategy (Blinky, Pinky, Inky and Clyde), while additional behaviours were implemented for **Frightened** and **Respawning** states. | Ghost movement became significantly more believable and challenging, providing a gameplay experience much closer to the original Pac-Man while keeping the AI modular and easy to extend. |
| **Layer Separation**                     | Rendering behaviour gradually started influencing gameplay decisions, making it difficult to determine whether collisions and movement should depend on interpolated or logical positions.                                                                 | Rendering was kept entirely independent from the simulation. The engine continues to operate exclusively on logical grid positions, while interpolation remains a presentation-only feature through renderer snapshots.                                                                                                        | The layered architecture was preserved, reducing coupling between the engine and the rendering layer and simplifying future maintenance.         |


## Lessons Learned

Several important lessons were learned throughout the development of the project:

- Defining the layered architecture before implementing gameplay mechanics significantly reduced coupling between the different subsystems and simplified future refactoring.
- Keeping the **Domain**, **Game Logic** and **Rendering** layers independent made the project easier to maintain, test and extend without affecting unrelated components.
- Simple algorithms are often sufficient to validate an idea, but progressively replacing them with more robust solutions (such as evolving from Manhattan-distance movement to **Breadth-First Search (BFS)** with ghost personalities) greatly improves the final gameplay experience.
- Gameplay systems should balance technical correctness with player perception. The collision system demonstrated that visual consistency can be just as important as the underlying implementation.
- Developing the rendering layer in parallel with the game engine accelerated implementation, while reinforcing the importance of defining clear interfaces between both subsystems.
- Continuous use of **flake8** and **mypy** helped maintain code quality throughout development, reducing technical debt and making refactoring considerably safer.
- Frequent communication between both developers ensured consistent architectural decisions and enabled different parts of the project to evolve in parallel without integration issues.
